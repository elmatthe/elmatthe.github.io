Overview

  • Two phases

    Years of saving with growing contributions, then years of inflation-adjusted withdrawals net of CPP, OAS or pension income.

  • Planner-ready outputs

    Probability of success, median values, ruin-year statistics, a percentile fan chart and checkpoint tables, in nominal or today's (real) dollars.

  • Private by design

    No market data or third-party service is used: the page, its chart library and every calculation come from this site and run on your device, and nothing you enter is sent anywhere.

    The model

    Annual return
    A simple return drawn independently each year from a normal distribution. Expected return is the arithmetic mean of those yearly returns; because of volatility, the typical (median) compound growth is lower, about exp(ln(1 + μ) − σ²/(2(1 + μ)²)) − 1, e.g. about 6.3% a year for 7% with 12% volatility. The form shows this figure.
    Very high volatility
    A normal distribution allows returns below −100%, which a real portfolio cannot have. Such a year is treated as a total loss (the balance becomes $0). The app warns when this affects at least 0.01% of years (for a 7% mean, from about 29% volatility) and whenever volatility is above 30%; it does not cap or change your inputs.
    Saving years
    value × (1 + return) + contribution, at the end of each year. Contributions grow by the contribution growth rate each year. A negative balance is floored at $0 but can recover.
    Spending basis
    Retirement-year dollars (the default, and how earlier versions worked): spending and pension are what you expect in the first year of retirement, in that year's prices. Today's dollars: they are converted first, first-year net withdrawal = (spending − pension) × (1 + inflation)years to retirement; the form shows the converted amount. Example: $60,000 − $18,000 = $42,000 today is $88,098 in 30 years at 2.5% inflation.
    Retirement years
    value × (1 + return) − withdrawal, at the end of each year (start-of-year withdrawals would give slightly lower success). The first-year net withdrawal grows with inflation every year, so the pension is effectively indexed too.
    Success
    A path fails (ruin) if its value reaches $0 or less during retirement (a plan that ends at exactly $0 counts as a failure); it stays at $0 afterwards. Probability of success = paths that never fail ÷ all paths.
    Percentiles
    10th, 25th, 50th, 75th and 90th percentiles of all paths for each year, using linear interpolation.
    Real dollars
    Nominal value ÷ (1 + inflation)year, i.e. today's purchasing power.
    Initial withdrawal rate
    First-year net withdrawal ÷ median portfolio at retirement.
    Median ruin year
    Among the paths that fail only: the median retirement year in which the money runs out.
    Random numbers
    A seeded generator (mulberry32 with the Box–Muller transform), used by both the web and the desktop app. The same seed and inputs give the same number of failed paths and the same percentiles in both, to within about 1 part in 1015 (the apps' math libraries can differ in the last digit); tested on Chromium/Node.js and CPython on Linux. Different browsers are expected, but not tested, to match equally closely.
    Exports
    The CSV lists all inputs, the spending basis, the seed, the generator, the engine version and the model, then every year's percentiles in nominal and real dollars, so any run can be reproduced.

    Troubleshooting

    “CPP/OAS/Pension income must be less than annual retirement spending”

    If guaranteed income already covers spending, the portfolio never needs to fund withdrawals, so there is nothing to simulate. Lower the pension figure or raise spending.

    Results change slightly every run

    Each run draws new random paths from a new seed. To reproduce a run exactly, copy the seed shown above the results into Random seed. Increase the number of simulations (up to 10,000) for more stable percentages.

    The chart is missing

    The chart uses a copy of Chart.js served by this site. If it did not load (for example a browser extension blocked it), reload the page; the summary tiles, table and CSV export work without it.

    My spending is in today's dollars

    Choose Today's dollars under Spending and pension are in. The form shows the first-year amount the model will use. Entering today's spending with the default (retirement-year dollars) understates later spending and overstates the probability of success.

    The desktop launcher stops with an error

    The launcher names the component that failed and keeps a log in files/test-logs/setup-and-run-latest.log. See the setup guide.
